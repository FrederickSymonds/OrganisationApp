# My System
## Context
I have created a system, based around two main concepts, life goals and actions.

**Life Goals:**
I firmly believe in the idea that life needs direction, which leads to the idea the conscious mind must aim at some higher goals, which must be perused with tenacity. These *life goals* are to stick around all your life, as such are not completely obtainable, but are a guide to where you should continually improve yourself in an area of life.

**Actions:**
We, as people however, can only take action in the present. As such I have also defined *actions* as a process, carried out by a person, that leads to a simple change. I gave them 4 categories (for my purposes);
- **Conscious** (Deliberate), Actions you know you intentionally choose to do.
- **Subconscious** (Unaware), Actions influenced by thoughts, habits or memories you aren't actively aware of.
- **Compulsive** (Addiction/Emotion), Actions driven by a strong urge or emotion that can feel difficult to resist.
- **Automatic** (Reflexes/ heart beat ...), Actions that occur without conscious decision or intentional control, often because the nervous system or body regulates them automatically.

Note:
- *There is some overlap between subconscious, compulsive and automatic, as in some sense they are all 'subconscious actions', by normal definitions of such.*
- *We shall ignore automatic from here on. Automatic actions are either in no need of improvement or are improved by a healthy lifestyle, which comes from following the system.*
## My system
This system is the result of combining the abstract, intangible life goals and real, tangible actions.
### Task System: (The bridge between conscious actions and life goals)
We need to link an abstract life goal, to what you decide to do right now. In order to do this, we need some sort of structure, that allows the two to meet. My proposal for this is this hierarchical structure;
1. **Life Goals:** These are some permanent, directions that can never be fully completed or checked off, but the pursuit of which enriches the life of the pursuer.
2. **Goals:** These are tied to a life goal. The completion of a goal is satisfaction based, you should feel that you have completed them, and the completion moves a person forward with respect to a life goal.
3. **Projects**: These are mainly tied to a goal, but can be tied to a life goal. The completion of these are objective based. They should be a complex objective, taking many tasks, many of which can be unknown at time of project conception.
4. **Tasks:** These are tied primarily to a project, but also a (life) goal. The completion of these are also objective based, however compared to projects, these are a string of actions that have an obvious start point, and can be achieved, with little consideration of how to progress, in order to finish it.

Note:
- *No mention of time, only life goals, of which you have your whole life to work towards. This is intentional, if we give a goal a 5 month lifespan, either in 5 months time it isn't completed/rushed, or was completed ages ago and such the lifespan has no point. There is no guessing correctly, as the amount of effort needed, before completion, in unknown.*
	- *However, to counter this, many events in life have a deadline, as such this is addressed below, and is given attention as a task type.*
	- *We also don't want to waste time, as such we aim to make as progress as possible, the main point of the system and as such the deadline has no meaning.*
- *Overall all of these stages differ by outcome, as you go more fundamental, the definition of a successful outcome is more vague and subjective, all the way down to a task, which has a defined outcome, and can say true and false to whether the task was completed.*

Tasks come in 3 types (This is done to simplify the process, nothing more);
- **Routine Tasks** - Tasks that occur at repeated intervals, these often combine together into a *routine*, (eg. morning routine).
- **Scheduled Tasks** - Tasks that have a deadline, preferable to do as soon as possible. 
- **Work Tasks** - Tasks that make progress directly.

Most days we should aim to make as much progress as possible, towards life goals, to which we must make progress towards goals, then projects which are all progressed by completing/progressing tasks. (How our brains turn a task into a bunch of conscious actions that then get it done is beyond my expertise, but it is stunning and amazing). 

Now a quick note on structuring the day, the best way, in my opinion, is to carve a day into blocks, each block is given some tasks to do, with gaps in between in order to relax and/or review. 

Next day's work and scheduled tasks should be decided the evening before, any earlier we can't say what is really needed to be done and any later, like in the morning, we are often not suited, as I have found that I generally choose easier and the wrong thing to do (however visualising the day, based on the tasks given is a good idea). 

To summarise how the system interacts with the task system, in reverse order (to give a different perspective), a day is given a bunch of blocks, every evening we choose what tasks fill next day's blocks. The tasks are stringed together, across many days with the purpose of finishing a project, or progressing a (life) goal. We then mash projects together in order to progress (life) goals, and we then finish goals, in order to progress life goals.

### Rule System: (The bridge between compulsive actions and life goals)
Here, we aim to gain control of your compulsive actions and bad habits. 

When faced with adversities, we can tend towards compulsive actions. When an outside event disrupts our work and flow, we can get tempted to give up or not return to the task at hand. If a task is hard or fills us with dread, we procrastinate. When we see a supermarket or store, we are tempted to go in and buy confectionery.

For this aspect of life, we need to gain control over the compulsion, to minimise and eventually kill it. However, we can't consciously do this, as out conscious minds aren't in control at this moment, and so must embed an idea deep into the subconscious mind. As such, we defin a rule in a similar vein;

**Rule:** A rule is a negotiation you make with your compulsive self, in order to reduce the amount of control it has over you. Sacrificing backwards progress, for control.

Rule Types;
- **Conditional Rules**, if 'X', do 'Y'.
	These are good for when a compulsive action leaves you unsure or confused how to act.
- **Constraint Rules**, limit 'X' to 'Y'.
	These are great for when a compulsive action is sucking too much time, money or energy.
- **Binary Rules**, always 'X', never 'Y'.
	Very strong and so should be reserved for when you have great existing control.
- **Sequential Rules**, first 'X', then 'Y'.
	These are great, in situations where you really don't want to do what you're supposed to, as are a direct trade off, saying do this good productive thing before doing the bad habit.

Rules should be relative based on where you are now. Looking at the confectionery example from earlier, you can impose the rule, if you give in, you must limit yourself to 2 items or limit to £3. If this is too restrictive (ie. you end up breaking it consistently), then make it less restrictive. If you are nailing it for a long time, make it more restrictive, until it has no control over you, and you have the binary rule, 'Never buy confectionery for yourself'. Ultimately you haggle with yourself, until you build a good habit, and have a strong, absolute rule surrounding it. Zooming out, all rules should aim to slowly castrate the parts of you that are opposing progress towards your life goals.

Rules themselves need rules. Not too many new ones at a given time. This is necessary as you are already at war with yourself, don't overextend, you will lose. Also rules should be committed to memory, and can be recited from it. You should really know deep down what your rules are and why you are following them, to be able to recite from memory and to reflect upon daily is a good way to do this. 

Finally, how do we actually incorporate them into the system? We need to track how progress on a rule is going and reflect on how a rule is going. To do this the system should track you many days since you last broke the rule. It should also allow for time to reflect when you have come close to breaking it and once you have broken it, which will be done through the review system (below). Reflecting on why you broke it, what led up to breaking it are essential in overcoming it, and being able to increase the number of days while of a rule not being broken should be satisfying.
### Review System (The bridge between subconscious actions and life goals)
The system is like a brick building, the task system is the bricks, the rule system is the scaffolding, to remove the restriction on how high it can go. However, we still need to review and assess whether or not the process so far is going according to plan. We need to take a step back and make sure that the original plans are being adhered to and everything is done in the correct order. This is the review system. The reason this links to subconscious actions, is because we speak to our subconscious here, we think through our feelings, intuitions, concerns over what we have done and think through them consciously, in order to make change that increases the rate of progress.

The review (or journal) system is basically what I've said above, we write out loud what we think in the back of our minds. If we have an idea of how to improve the method, we put it here, if we have an idea about wasted effort, then we write it down and if we are concerned with direction project is going in, we write it up here. The reason I say 'journal' in brackets, is also the review system can also comment on days, weeks, months ... and general feelings on life progress, happiness and these can then influence how I approach the life goals themselves.

Review Types:
**Life Goals, Goals, Projects, Tasks:**
- These are reviews on progress, linked to a specific goal or project. These can inform future decisions or make changes in current approaches.
**Day, Week, Month, ...:**
- These are journal entries/reviews on how life is coming along, in terms of progress and where it is going, over the time span, said in the title.
**Rule:**
- As alluded to earlier, these are comments about rules, when I come close to or have broken, to try and get to the crux of the problem, to get ahead of the problem.
